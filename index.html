Vision-Why now

AI workloads have changed. Long-context and agentic inference now dominate, and their cost is set less by arithmetic than by moving data between memory, chips and racks. Most silicon shipping today was designed before that shift.

NAIRI is designing for it from the start: an accelerator and rack-scale system co-designed as one, programmable without us, so the capacity it produces is as fungible as a GPU-hour.

We're building it in Armenia, which has trained chip designers since the early 2000s and has a growing machine-learning bench. Those are the two halves this kind of system needs.

System-principles

  1. Co-design-Principle 01

    Chip and rack, designed together.

    Compute, memory hierarchy, interconnect and software are specified against one another rather than integrated after the fact.

  2. Data movement-Principle 02

    Built around data movement.

    Where the bytes live, how far they travel and how often: that's what the architecture is chosen against.

  3. Open-Principle 03

    Programmable without us.

    A system any team can target with their own models, not one that needs our engineers for every new workload.

  4. Simulation-Principle 04

    Proven in simulation first.

    Our in-house simulation environment lets us compare architectures against real workloads before committing to silicon.
